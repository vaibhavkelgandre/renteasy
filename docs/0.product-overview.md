# RentEasy — product overview

A two-sided marketplace for renting physical things: bikes, cameras, water purifiers, power tools,
apartments. Anyone can list what they own; anyone can rent what they need, **by the hour, the day or
the month**. Prices are negotiable.

This page exists so every feature document has the same picture of the product. It is context, not a
spec — the specs live in [`features/`](features/).

---

## 1. The one-line description

> **Anyone can rent out anything they own, and anyone can rent it — hourly, daily or monthly, at a
> price they can negotiate.**

## 2. What makes this architecturally different

Worth stating early, because it drives almost every design decision below.

### There is no role hierarchy

There is no OWNER role and no RENTER role. **The same person does both**, often on the same day —
listing a camera in the morning and renting a bike in the afternoon.

So authorization is **relationship-based, not role-based**:

```
Can I edit this listing?      → am I its owner?
Can I see this booking?       → am I its owner OR its renter?
Can I accept this offer?      → am I the owner of the listing it is for?
Can I review this person?     → did I complete a booking with them?
```

The only real role in the system is `ADMIN` — platform staff, who are not marketplace participants at
all. Everyone else is just a **user**, and what they may do depends entirely on which record they are
touching.

**Practical consequence:** there is no `requireRole` gate on most endpoints. There is a
`requireOwnership`-style check inside each service, next to the data. A role gate would answer the
wrong question.

### Money actually moves

Rent, a refundable security deposit, and a platform commission. That means:

- **Every amount is integer paise.** A float in a money path is a bug.
- Deposits are **held and returned**, not earned — a different lifecycle from rent.
- The platform's cut has to be computed and recorded, not implied.
- Payouts to owners happen *after* a rental completes, not at booking.

### Two things can be true at once, and must not be

**The same item cannot be rented to two people for overlapping periods.** That is the hardest
correctness problem in the product, and it is a genuine concurrency problem — two people can hit
"Book" on the same camera for the same weekend in the same millisecond.

## 3. The core objects

```
User ──owns──▶ Listing ──has──▶ Availability (calendar, blackout dates)
                  │
                  ├──▶ RateCard        hourly / daily / monthly prices
                  │
                  └──▶ Booking ◀──made by── User (the renter)
                         │
                         ├──▶ Offer / Counter-offer   the negotiation
                         ├──▶ Payment                 rent + deposit + commission
                         └──▶ Review                  both directions, after completion
```

## 4. The rental unit problem

A listing carries up to three prices — hourly, daily, monthly — and **the system charges the
cheapest applicable combination**, not a naive multiplication.

```
Camera:  ₹150/hour · ₹800/day · ₹15,000/month

Rent for 6 hours   → 6 × 150            = ₹900        …but a day is ₹800, so charge ₹800
Rent for 30 days   → 30 × 800           = ₹24,000     …but a month is ₹15,000, so charge ₹15,000
Rent for 40 days   → 1 month + 10 days  = ₹23,000
```

**This must be computed server-side and shown before booking**, itemised. A renter who is charged
₹24,000 when the monthly rate would have been ₹15,000 will not come back, and a platform that
silently overcharges is not one anybody trusts.

## 5. Negotiation

A renter can offer a different price. The owner accepts, rejects, or counters.

```
Renter offers ₹600/day  ──▶  Owner counters ₹700/day  ──▶  Renter accepts  ──▶  Booking at ₹700/day
```

Rules that matter:

- **An accepted price is frozen into the booking.** The listing's rate card can change tomorrow; a
  confirmed booking cannot.
- **Offers expire**, or an owner's inbox fills with stale offers on dates that have since been booked.
- **An offer does not hold the dates.** Only a confirmed booking does — otherwise anyone could block
  an item indefinitely by offering ₹1.
- Negotiation is per-booking-request, not per-listing.

## 6. Trust, because a stranger is taking your ₹80,000 camera

The features that make a marketplace work at all, in the order they matter:

| | Why |
|---|---|
| **Email verification** | Required before listing or booking. Browsing needs no account at all. |
| **Security deposit** | Held at booking, returned after the item comes back. The main protection. |
| **Two-way reviews** | Owners rate renters and renters rate owners, after completion only. |
| **Phone verification** | Higher trust tier; gates high-value listings. |
| **ID verification (KYC)** | Later. Needed above a value threshold in a real deployment. |
| **Dispute handling** | Damage, late return, no-show. Admin-mediated. |

## 7. Categories are not all the same

An apartment is not a camera, and pretending otherwise produces a product that fits neither:

| | Camera / tools | Bike / vehicle | Apartment / house |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical unit | hourly, daily | daily | monthly |
| Handover | in person or delivery | in person | in person, keys |
| Deposit | high relative to rent | medium | very high |
| Regulation | none | licence check | rental agreement, local law |

**V1 handles portable goods** — cameras, bikes, tools, appliances. Property is deliberately deferred:
it needs agreements, longer terms and legal compliance that would swamp everything else.

## 8. What V1 is, and is not

**In:** registration and verification · listings with rate cards and availability · search and browse
· booking requests · negotiation · confirmed bookings with no double-booking · handover and return ·
two-way reviews.

**Deferred, deliberately:** real payment gateway integration (the money *model* exists; the gateway
comes later, and an amount is never marked paid because the frontend said so — only on server-side
verification of the provider's webhook) · delivery and logistics · property rentals · insurance ·
disputes beyond a basic admin flag · chat.
